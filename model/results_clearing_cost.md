## 14. What the numbers say

1. Access funding is small in expectation: 2.0 bps at base against 19 bps of credit, and only 9 bps with no advance dividend and D = 3. The bridge matters as cash at trigger ($500M on $1,000M), not as an expected cost; the standing-capacity term k, paid every year whether or not anything fails, is about 5 times the expected access cost for the funded vehicle.
2. At base the transfer cost (k + f = 25 bps) is larger than the risk itself (credit + access = 21 bps). At a low PD, most of the price of protection is the cost of the transfer.
3. Provider c* at base: funded vehicle 46, bank SBLC 49, pool 46 bps. For the bank the full P price (49) slightly exceeds the Q price (48), so capital rather than the risk premium sets its price at low PD; the P price binds below lambda_P of about 0.28%. The P floor (EL_P + capital) is 22 bps, of which capital is 15. At the 100% stress outflow the bank's c* is 77 bps.
4. Pool protection is worth 3.8 bps less to the depositor at base (range 2.8 to 4.7). The haircut is small in bps because it scales with lambda_Q; its content is the tail (Ohio and Maryland, 1985), where the pool fails when it is needed. A 10% reserve cannot pay par on a failed member whose covered balance exceeds 10% of total pool coverage without post-event assessments.
5. Bearing the risk versus buying protection. With a 50 bps yield advantage the depositor is paid for bearing the risk up to lambda_P of about 0.60%, but buying protection from the vehicle pays only up to 0.30% (bank 0.28%). At 100 bps: bearing 1.21%, buying 0.90%. At 25 bps the depositor is paid for bearing the risk up to 0.30% but no protection purchase clears, because k + f alone are 25 bps. At zero or negative advantage (an earnings credit rate) neither clears at any PD.
6. So a depositor can be fairly paid for the risk and still rationally decline to buy protection; the 25 bps transfer cost is the space a private provider has to compress.
7. The base-case lambda_Q of 0.75% corresponds to a senior spread near 45 bps at 60% senior LGD. Hypothetical spreads of 60 to 250 bps imply lambda_Q of 1.00% to 4.17% (mu of 4 to 17 against lambda_P 0.25%), and a credit component at LGD 25% of 25 to 104 bps. As an upper reference, spread-implied pricing sits far above the base case once a bank trades wide, which is when depositors would want protection.
8. Biggest single drivers (funded vehicle): lambda_P (33 to 191 bps across 0.10% to 2.00%), then mu (2 to 5: 39 to 60) and LGD (15% to 40%: 39 to 56). a and D move c* by at most 4.0 bps.
9. The wedge: $1,000M of cash at trigger, $500M net after a 50% advance dividend, $150M to $400M ultimate loss across LGD 15% to 40%, against about $4.6M a year of premium at base; one event costs roughly 60 years of premium at LGD 25%.
10. Tranche A. The first loss earns 83 bps over T-bills against a P-expected loss of 25 bps, an excess spread of 58 bps a year, for a position that loses 100% of principal when the one bank fails. At lambda_P 1% the numbers scale to 332 / 100 / 232. The excess is almost entirely the mu - 1 risk premium: with mu = 1 the spread would be 28 bps and the excess 2.7 bps. It is thin against the event: an all-or-nothing, single-name outcome that no amount of spread diversifies inside a one-bank vehicle.
11. The senior earns 13 bps for standing ready; at failure it gets 67% of principal back within days and has 33% locked for about 2 years without interest (opportunity cost about 2.9% of principal). It loses principal only if realized LGD exceeds the thickness: at 40% it loses 20%. Sizing the first loss at a stressed 40% instead of 25% cuts the first-loss spread to 52 bps and the excess to 36.
12. Portfolio B. Clustering leaves expected loss unchanged (0.0625% of the portfolio at every rho) and moves the tail. P(3 or more failures among 50) is 0.03% under independence, 0.19% at rho 0.10 and 0.84% at rho 0.30. The 99.5th percentile loss goes from 1.0% (2 failures) to 1.5% (3) at rho 0.30.
13. Tranche losses move the same way: the mezzanine's probability of any loss goes from 0.03% (independence) to 0.84% at rho 0.30, and the senior's from 0.000% to 0.063%. Diversification makes each depositor's protection cheaper to back, but the capacity needed is set by clusters, and a provider covering the class would need cash for the 3 failures of a 99.5th-percentile year at rho 0.30: $3B of gross cash at trigger if each exposure is $1B. Private protection can make one depositor safe; it cannot make uninsured deposits safe as a class without capacity for the cluster.
14. Recovery gap C. Buying the remaining claim at 90% of expected recovery returns 11.1% a year over one year, 5.4% over two and 3.6% over three; at 95% the two-year return (2.6%) is below r (4.3%). The return is all discount and all duration risk. A buyer at 90% gets only its price back at LGD 27.5%, so a 2.5 percentage-point miss on LGD erases the return; at LGD 40% the buyer loses 56% of its price. This is the same bridge the tranche-A senior carries.

Immaterial in size (access 2.0 bps on notional).
14. v3 flag, thickness = expected LGD. A first loss sized at the expected 25% leaves the senior exposed to any LGD miss, which is exactly the uncertainty the recovery-gap analysis shows. Section 10 shows the 40% thickness alternative.
15. v3 flag, portfolio tranche points. # Part II. A partially funded multi-bank vehicle

## 16. Design, cash timing and conventions

The vehicle writes protection on 50 reference banks, each capped at 2% of covered notional (one failure = a par payment of 2% of the pool). Failures follow a one-factor Gaussian copula with asset correlation rho; z is the common factor and a low z is a bad year for every bank at once.

Cash timing on one failure, per unit of that bank's covered balance:

| When | Cash | Source |
|---|---|---|
| T+0 (failure, receiver appointed, usually a Friday) | vehicle pays par: 1 | design |
| T+0 + about 7 days | advance dividend a comes back (base 50%, range 30% to 60%) | assumption based on FDIC practice: advance dividends since 1984; IndyMac 50%; SVB announcement said 'within the week' |
| Over D years (base 2, range 1 to 3) | remaining recovery 1 - a - LGD comes back | author assumption |
| Never | LGD is the final loss | author assumption |

Liabilities and waterfall. (1) A funded buffer theta, invested in T-bills, pays par first and is the first loss; it also carries the term bridge after the advance dividend. (2) A committed liquidity facility F is drawn at T+0 only for the par payments above the buffer, and is repaid from the advance dividends. (3) If the advance dividends do not repay it (term need n w (1 - a) above theta), the facility terms out for up to D years, secured on the receivership claims; it takes a credit loss only if the pool's credit loss exceeds theta. (4) If T+0 cash exceeds theta + F the vehicle cannot pay par: **protection failure**. Shortfalls are shared pro rata across the failed names in the window; an unpaid depositor keeps its deposit claim and bears LGD on the unpaid share.

Design standard for timing: **simultaneous**. All of a year's failures are assumed to land inside one advance-dividend window, so T+0 cash = n x 2%. This is the conservative bound; spring 2023 had two failures over one weekend. The sequential alternative (failures more than a week apart, so earlier advance dividends are back) needs w + (n - 1) w (1 - a) at the last failure and is shown as a sensitivity.

Measures. Protection-failure, facility-draw and vehicle-default probabilities are real-world (P) probabilities at lambda_P. Prices are under Q: the same copula run at mu x lambda_P per name (same rho, same LGD mapping), which with a fixed LGD gives exactly the single-name credit (18.75 bps) and access (1.99 bps) terms. Probabilities are exact (quadrature over z, 4,001 points; Student-t variant over a 401 x 240 grid in (z, W)); a seeded latent-variable Monte Carlo (1,000,000 years, seed 20261018) checks them in Section 18.

Pricing. k_pool = theta x s_lock + F x fee_F + expected drawn cost; c*_pool = credit_Q + access_Q + k_pool + f. Expected drawn cost = E_Q[T+0 draw] x (r + s_draw) x lag / 365 + E_Q[term-out] x (s_draw - s_b) x D; the r part of the term-out is already in the access term. The facility's share of credit loss (loss above theta) is part of the credit term, not an extra charge.

## 17. A1. Correlated LGD and the advance dividend

## 21. A5. The facility provider: cost floor and wrong-way risk

(i) A bank providing the facility. On my reading of 12 CFR 249.32(e), a committed liquidity facility to a special purpose entity or financial sector entity carries a 100% LCR outflow, so the bank holds HQLA equal to the undrawn amount at the 40 bps net carry used in the model. Capital: base 100% CCF x the SPE's risk weight (100%, an unrated SPE treated as a corporate exposure) x 13.5% CET1 x (10% hurdle - 4.3% r). If the facility were treated as an ordinary commitment the CCF would be 20% (one year or less) or 50% (over one year); as a senior securitization exposure the risk weight could fall toward its 20% floor. These are floors: they exclude the provider's expected loss, lending-limit usage and any return for the wrong-way position.

| Treatment (on my reading) | LCR cost | Capital cost | Floor on fee_F |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% outflow; 100% CCF x 100% RW (base) | 40.0 | 77.0 | 117.0 |
| 100% outflow; 50% CCF x 100% RW | 40.0 | 38.5 | 78.5 |
| 100% outflow; 20% CCF x 100% RW | 40.0 | 15.4 | 55.4 |
| 100% outflow; 100% CCF x 20% RW | 40.0 | 15.4 | 55.4 |

So a bank-provided facility costs at least 55.4 to 117.0 bps a year on the undrawn amount.

(ii) A non-bank provider (an insurer or asset manager committing to repo its own Treasury holdings to the vehicle, or to buy the vehicle's receivership claims at a pre-agreed price). There is no verified market price. The provider bears no LCR or bank capital charge, but must keep the Treasuries unencumbered for the life of the commitment, must deliver cash in a banking crisis, and takes as collateral receivership claims, which are not standard repo collateral. Its cost is swept at 5, 10, 20 and 40 bps, with 20 bps as the mid case. The structure must also survive the provider's own stress: insurers and asset managers face redemptions and margin calls in the same crisis.

## 29. What the v5 numbers say

1. Pooled c*. At theta 10% and F 10%, the pooled vehicle costs 38.7 bps with a 20 bps non-bank facility, against 45.7 for the single-name vehicle; across theta 5% to 20% and fee_F 5 to 40 bps it spans 37.0 to 42.3 bps (f 15). The saving is the capacity term only: credit and access are the same per bank, so pooling can save at most the 10 bps of lock premium, and gives part of it back in facility fees.
2. Break-even fee. With equal frictions, the pool beats 45.7 bps up to a fee of 90 bps at theta 10% (F 10%) and 63 bps at theta 5% (F 15%). A bank facility at its cost floor (55.4 to 117.0 bps on my reading of LCR and capital) sits around or above that.
3. Facility sizing. Committing the facility on 90% of notional at a 20 bps fee gives 54.7; at a bank floor 86.6 to 142.0. Sized to need (10%) at 20 bps it is 38.7, and about 31 bps is reachable only by also cutting frictions to 7.5 (31.2). So about 31 bps is a defensible low end only if both the frictions and the facility cost assumptions are evidenced; the base is 38 to 39.
4. Protection failure. At theta 10% + F 10% the vehicle cannot pay par with probability 0.0097% a year (1 in 10,326) at rho 0.30 under a Gaussian copula, but 0.21% under a t(5) copula and 0.0443% at PD 0.50%. Conditional on the depositor's own bank failing it is 1.01%. With only the buffer (a facility unavailable in stress) it is 0.11%. The tail model moves the answer more than any parameter.
5. The facility as the weak link. If the provider is a bank (PD 0.10%, rho 0.30) its conditional PD in the years it is drawn is 5.7%, and P(cannot pay par) rises from 0.0097% to 0.0147%; the value haircut is a fraction of a basis point, but it is concentrated in exactly the cluster years.
6. Stress. A 2023-like year (3 failures) needs 6% at T+0, inside a 10% buffer, and loses 1.5%. Six failures at LGD 40% and a 30% advance dividend need 12% at T+0 (facility 2%); the advance dividends repay the facility within the week, the buffer carries the 8.4% term bridge, and the credit loss is 4.8%. Ten failures need 20%: par is paid only with F of at least 10%, the facility terms out 4.0%, and the credit loss (8.0%) still fits inside the buffer.
7. Who pays changes the posted rate, not the cost: with protection, depositor net yield plus provider income always equals the bank's all-in cost of the balance.

v5 modelling notes (specification issues and choices):

1. The T+0 protection-failure event depends only on the failure count under the simultaneous standard, so correlated LGD does not move it. LGD enters through term-out and loss-above-buffer (fig-07 right panel), and through the advance dividend, which I linked to LGD (a = (2/3)(1 - LGD)) in the correlated mode [author assumption].
2. Prices use the copula under Q (PD x mu). Pricing each tranche at mu 3 times its own P-EL would understate senior tranches (Section 24).
3. The facility's credit loss (loss above theta) is allocated from the credit term, not added; adding it would double count.
4. The design rule theta + F = 20% (10 simultaneous failures) is mine, chosen so the vehicle's P-measure default probability is about 0.01% a year at rho 0.30 under a Gaussian copula. It is a judgment for a sponsor and a rating agency.
5. Bank facility cost floor uses a 100% CCF and a 100% risk weight in the base reading. A 20% or 50% CCF (ordinary commitment) or a 20% securitization floor gives 55.4 to 78.5 bps. All on my reading; counsel should confirm.
6. A repo against FDIC advance-dividend receivables has no standard collateral market; I modelled the facility as unsecured liquidity repaid from advance dividends, then secured on receivership claims if it terms out.
7. Rho, the LGD mapping, the provider PD and s_draw are illustrative, not calibrated.

## 30. Conventions for Part III

Only the commitment fee is saved; amounts drawn from the same-day source are charged at the facility's drawn cost.
- Market-depth caveat. The extra same-day cash is uncommitted. Protection failure is therefore shown twice: if that depth is there in the stress year (capacity unchanged, so the v6 probability), and if it is not (capacity = theta + committed F). ## 33. C. Bank willingness to pay, bank-sponsored variant (illustrative)

WTP = dP_run x PV(relationship). V is the relationship value in bps a year of the covered balance (net interest margin on the deposit, fees, cross-sell). PV = V x annuity factor over L = 5 years at r + 3% = 7.3%, end-of-year payments: factor 4.067. dP_run is the cut in the annual probability that this depositor leaves in a run. The bank pays a share s = 50% of c*. Rational when WTP >= s x c*. Every input is a sweep point, not a calibration; the run-cost savings to the bank (liquidity held against the balance, fire-sale risk) are left out, so WTP is a floor on that count.

## 34. What the v7 numbers say

1. Frictions move the price more than the facility. At the pooled mid design, f from 15 to 10, 7.5 and 5 bps takes c* from 38.7 to 33.7, 31.2 and 28.7 bps; the single-name vehicle goes from 45.7 to 35.7. Dropping the committed facility altogether saves 2.0 bps.
2. The 34 bps marker is the mid design at f 10 (33.7); the 31 bps marker needs f 7.5 (31.2), or f 9.3 with no committed facility. The single-name vehicle would need f 0.3 for 31 and 3.3 for 34.
3. Mobility is a liquidity trade, not a price trade. Cutting F from 10% to 0 leaves P(cannot pay par) at 0.0097% only if the same-day market is there in the cluster year; without it the probability rises to 0.11% (1 in 923).
4. Investors: the pooled equity earns 9.961% a year over its expected loss with a 9.83% chance of any loss, 9.96% of its tail loss (ES); the single-name first loss earns 0.580% excess, 1.16% of its ES. Per unit of tail the senior (8.16%) is close to the equity, but on 0.028% of excess and a 0.3% ES; the mezzanine (3.07%) and the single-name first loss trail.
5. Bank sponsorship is rational against the pooled mid price only at V 100 with dP_run 5.0%, V 150 with dP_run 5.0%. At V 100 the break-even dP_run is 4.8% a year.

v7 modelling notes (specification issues and choices):

1. Buffer mobility: v6 already counts the buffer at par as T+0 cash, so a mobile buffer cannot add capacity under the v6 accounting on its own. The extra m x theta is an author assumption (uncommitted same-day cash, net of haircut) and the 'no depth' column is its honest downside.
2. Pooled tranche spreads are Q-expected loss only, as modelled in Section 24; the lock premium and access are not allocated to tranches. Spreads for a real deal would be set by the market.
3. WTP omits the bank's own run-cost savings and any brokered-deposit or implicit-support cost.

Weekend and after-hours triggers with conventional bills. Under the term sheet the vehicle has a stated number of business days to pay, and T-bills sell on any of them, so the buffer is cash. A vehicle that promised par before markets reopen after a Friday-evening trigger could not count conventional bills: with no facility its capacity before reopening is zero, so it could pay nothing on such a trigger, and that would happen in every year with a failure: P(1+ failures) = 8.59% a year (t(5): 4.06%), and 100% conditional on the depositor's own bank failing. The hybrid's facility alone would cover 5 failures: 0.11% a year (t(5): 0.57%; 6.55% given the own bank fails). A tokenized buffer that is cash at any hour restores the full 20% before reopening (0.0097% Gaussian, 0.21% t(5)), provided the depth is there. So the all-funded design should promise par within stated business days, not before reopening, unless its buffer is mobile.

## 42. Exact cash-flow check of the four-term formula

Exact: constant hazard h = -ln(1 - lambda_Q) = 0.7528% (lambda_Q 0.75% is a one-year probability in the formula); premium paid continuously until default or expiry and discounted at r; at default the provider pays par, receives the advance dividend after 5 business days (5/252 years) and the remaining recovery after D = 2 years, each bridge financed at r + s_b = 5.3% with continuous discounting. With a running premium the premium and protection legs share the survival discount, so the exact spread is h x (cost per default valued at the default date). k and f are running charges on the surviving covered balance in both. For the pooled design with fixed LGD the credit and access terms are the same expectation per name, so the per-name exact terms apply; the all-funded design has no drawn cost.

The four-term formula sits within 0.02 bps of full discounting: the hazard conversion adds to the credit term, while simple interest on the certificate bridge slightly overstates the access term and the 5-day advance-dividend bridge, which the formula omits, adds a little back. ## 44. Settlement regimes at the trigger, and the banker's term-premium value (pilot hypothesis)

Three ways the vehicle can settle when a reference bank fails, 50 banks at 2% each, rho 0.30, PD 0.25%, correlated LGD, reliability conditional on the depositor's own bank failing (as Sections 38 and 39). Gross par: the vehicle buys the whole claim at par at T+0 and collects the advance dividend; each failure draws 2.0% of pool notional. Net of dividend: the depositor collects the FDIC advance dividend a directly and the vehicle pays par less the dividend actually paid at day 7 against the receivership certificate; each failure draws 2% x (1 - a), and a is state-dependent (a = (2/3)(1 - LGD), 40% in the worst states, 50% at the base LGD), so the draw rises exactly in the states that produce shortfalls; with no dividend the rule collapses to gross par. The figures assume the dividend has been paid by day seven; a later payment pushes that failure's draw back toward the gross-par row. Junior slice: the vehicle pays a fixed 40% of par in cash at day 7, takes the recoveries above 60% of par and pays a carry coupon at r + s_b on the depositor's outstanding certificate; each failure draws 0.80% of pool notional; the vehicle bears LGD up to 40% and the depositor any excess. The price c* is the all-funded price at the regime's buffer: the credit term is unchanged under net (the vehicle still bears the whole LGD) and under the junior slice while LGD stays at or below 40%, which holds throughout the correlated mapping (15% to 40%); the access term is the same certificate carry in every regime. What changes is the depositor's position: under gross par it holds cash at T+0; under net it holds the dividend when the FDIC pays it (usually within 30 days, at the FDIC's discretion) and the rest at day 7; under the junior slice it holds the dividend, the cash slice and a senior claim on recoveries up to 60%.
