# Interactive dashboards of "Cash That Never Sleeps"

Two single self-contained pages (no external scripts, fonts or network calls), one for each clock of the manual. Each links to the other in its header. Every parameter on both is illustrative and calibrated to no bank; the defaults are the base cases of the engines in `model/`, and each page lists its formulas in a Method panel.

## The run clock: `index.html` (Chapter 3)

Change the parameters of Chapter 3 and see the results recompute in your browser: the four-term clearing cost (credit, access, capacity, frictions) for the single-name funded vehicle and the pooled vehicle, how often the pool cannot pay par each year, the probability and severity of a shortfall in a year when the depositor's own bank fails under Gaussian and Student-t copulas, conditional shortfall against capacity, the buffer-versus-facility rule, and the default probability at which the price equals a given yield advantage. The defaults are the base case of `model/clearing_cost.py`. The formulas are in the notation of Chapter 3.

The page's math lives in one plain script block (`<script id="model">`) and is tested against the Python workpaper `model/clearing_cost.py`. `python3 -I tests/fixtures.py` imports the workpaper and writes its outputs for the base case and twelve varied parameter sets to `tests/fixtures.json`; `node tests/parity.mjs` evaluates the page's script and requires agreement within 0.05 basis points on prices and 2 percent relative (or 1e-6 absolute) on probabilities, and checks the headline numbers of Chapter 3. The page uses coarser quadrature grids than the workpaper for speed; the test confirms the two agree.

## The settlement clock: `settlement.html` (Chapter 6)

Change the parameters of Chapter 6 and see the settlement clock recompute: the always-on collateralized netting facility (R7) against the prefunded joint account (R9) in four terms, low, high and base, with the lendable share and the S2 verdict at any bar; the share at which the two cross; the Sunday settlement cash source (case a, b1 or b2, or a mix of b1 or b2 with loans already pledged) and the share of the Sunday capacity at which R9 and R7 tie; the daylight overdraft fee at the Sunday opening; how often a member's cap binds per weekend and per year, the backing a 1 percent or once-in-ten-years bind needs, and the largest median weekend peak net debit at which the lendable screen and a 1 percent bind hold together (1.56 percent of the balance at sigma 0.8); R9's rejection rate and rejected amount without a weekend top-up; the same-weekend count distribution of an open debtor cohort under Gaussian and Student-t(5) factors and the joint cap ladder; the closed-window calendar of 2026 and 2027 under today's Fedwire Funds schedule, the announced 22x6 schedule and a seven-day schedule, with linear or square-root scaling of the peak debit with the window hours; and the price by deposit type of Section 10b of `model/results_routes.md`. The defaults are the base case of `model/settlement.py` and `model/routes.py`.

Everything on the page recomputes in the browser except two kinds of value, each labelled where it appears. R6 (the mutual with a Treasury-backed tail, 21.03 bps at a lendable share of 0.97) and the strict R5 row (37.94 at 1.00) are fixed inputs from the mutual-tail and clearing-cost engines. The queued amounts of the open debtor cohort come from the settlement engine's Monte Carlo and are shown as the engine's published values at the base case, not recomputed. The same-weekend counts and the joint cap ladder are computed live by quadrature over the factor, as in the engine, on coarser grids for speed.

The page's math lives in one plain script block (`<script id="model">`) and is tested against the engines. `python -I -B tests/fixtures_settlement.py` imports `model/settlement.py` (which loads `model/routes.py` and its engines; several minutes, nothing written to `model/`), sets the engines' parameters in memory for the base case and nine varied parameter sets, re-runs the engines' own code for the blocks that depend on them, and writes the outputs to `tests/fixtures_settlement.json`. It needs numpy. `node tests/parity_settlement.mjs` evaluates the page's script and requires agreement within 0.05 basis points on prices and 2 percent relative (or 1e-6 absolute) on probabilities and shares, checks that the fixtures' base case reproduces the engines' published values, and checks the headline settlement numbers of Chapter 6.
