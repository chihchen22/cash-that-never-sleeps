1. Clearing cost decomposition

Basis points a year on the covered balance. Credit and access are the risk the depositor bears unprotected; capacity and frictions are the cost of transferring it.

2. When the pool fails

Real-world probabilities. Simultaneous timing standard: all of a year's failures land inside one advance-dividend window, so the cash needed at the trigger is the number of failures times each bank's share.

Expected loss uses the LGD set in the inputs. Section 38 of the workpaper's results file uses the state-dependent LGD (switch it on to compare); the shortfall and severity do not depend on LGD.

3. Shortfall given the depositor's own bank fails, by capacity

Probability that the pool cannot pay par in a year in which the depositor's own bank fails, against capacity θ + F (logarithmic scale). The vertical line marks the current design.

Table view

4. Buffer versus facility

At fixed capacity, replacing one unit of funded buffer with one unit of committed facility changes the price by the facility fee plus its expected drawn cost per unit, minus the buffer's lock-up premium.

5. Break-even yield advantage

The bank default probability λP at which the clearing cost equals the yield advantage Δ the depositor earns by holding the balance at the bank (set Δ in the inputs). Below it, protection costs less than the yield advantage.

Method: the formulas used, in the notation of Chapter 3

Single-name clearing cost (four terms)

c* = λQ LGDdep + λQ max(0, 1 − a − LGDdep) (r + sb) D + k + f,   λQ = μ λP

Credit, access, standing capacity, frictions. Credit plus access is the risk (what the depositor bears unprotected); k + f is the transfer cost. The single-name vehicle is fully funded, so it never falls short.

Pooled vehicle price

c*pool = creditQ + accessQ + θ slock + F feeF + expected drawn cost + f

N reference banks, each w = 1/N of covered notional; n failures in the year. Expectations are on the pricing measure: the copula is run with the per-bank probability μ λP (capped at 0.999), same ρ, same LGD mapping. creditQ = EQ[n w LGD]; accessQ = EQ[n w max(0, 1 − a − LGD)] (r + sb) D. With a fixed LGD these equal the single-name credit and access terms exactly.

drawn = EQ[min(max(n w − θ, 0), F)] (r + sdraw) lag/365 + EQ[min(max(n w (1 − a) − θ, 0), F)] max(0, sdraw − sb) D

The first part is the facility drawn from the trigger until the advance dividend; the second is what is still drawn after it and terms out for up to D years.

Copulas and the failure count

Gaussian: p(z) = Φ( (Φ−1(λ) − √ρ z) / √(1 − ρ) ),   z ~ N(0, 1)
Student-t(ν): p(z, W) = Φ( (c √(W/ν) − √ρ z) / √(1 − ρ) ),   W ~ χ²ν,  EW[Φ(c √(W/ν))] = λ
n | common factor ~ Binomial(N, p)

Every probability is computed by exact quadrature over the common factor, on grids coarser than the workpaper's, for speed: 2,001 equally spaced points on [−8, 8] for the Gaussian copula (workpaper 4,001), and a 161 × 120 grid in (z, W) for the Student-t (workpaper 401 × 240), with W on a log grid from 10−4 to 80. On the tested parameter sets the results agree with the workpaper to better than 0.01 percent relative. Binomial terms smaller than 10−16 of the largest term in their row are dropped.

When the pool fails

P(cannot pay par) = P(n w > θ + F)   on the real-world measure (λ = λP)

Given that a named bank (the depositor's own) fails, each state is weighted by p times the Binomial(N − 1, p) distribution of the other banks' failures, and n = 1 + the others. Shortfall is P(n w > θ + F | own bank fails). A shortfall is shared pro rata across the failed names, so the unpaid share is max(0, 1 − (θ + F)/(n w)); severity is its expectation given a shortfall, and expected loss is E[unpaid share × LGD | own bank fails], since an unpaid depositor keeps its deposit claim and bears LGD on it.

Buffer versus facility

Δc* per unit of buffer swapped for facility = feeF + (expected drawn cost)/F − slock   (capacity θ + F fixed)

The facility carries no receivership credit exposure at full use of the capacity when θ ≥ (1 − a)(θ + F): the advance dividends then repay it within the week.

Break-even

The λP at which c*(λP) = Δ, searched on 0 to 20 percent. "None" means the price exceeds Δ at any default probability (the transfer cost alone is above Δ).

State-dependent LGD (optional)

LGD(z) = 15% + 25% × min(1, max(0, z / −3)),   a(z) = (2/3)(1 − LGD(z))

LGD rises from 15 percent in a normal year to 40 percent when the common factor is three standard deviations below its mean, and the advance dividend falls with it. Under the Student-t copula the mapping reads the Gaussian factor z. When it is on, the LGD and advance-dividend inputs apply to the single-name vehicle only.

What this page does not do

  • Only the simultaneous timing standard is computed; the workpaper's sequential alternative is not.
  • The facility is assumed to perform: no provider default on the common factor and no material-adverse-change clause. Both make the hybrid fail more often than shown.
  • One correlation is used for prices and probabilities. The workpaper prices at ρ = 0.20 and states failure statistics at 0.30; with a fixed LGD the price does not depend on ρ except through the facility's drawn cost, a few thousandths of a basis point at base.
  • No Year-2, replenishment, tranche or bank-seller calculations; those are in the workpaper.

The JavaScript on this page is tested against the Python workpaper on the base case and twelve varied parameter sets (prices within 0.05 basis points, probabilities within 2 percent relative).
