2. The Sunday settlement cash source

At the Sunday 9:00 p.m. Fedwire Funds opening every net debtor settles in reserves, so it needs reserves or collateralized Reserve Bank credit of at least its cap. R7's published price assumes case (a). Case (b1) adds R7's encumbrance spread on a further share; case (b2) adds R9's reserve convention (loan spread over bills plus the bill yield over IORB).

Daylight overdraft fee at the Sunday opening

The overdraft runs from Sunday 21:00 to Monday 12:00 (15 hours), repaid from a delivery-versus-payment sale or repo of the bills after the 8:30 a.m. securities opening; fee on the policy's 24-hour day and 360-day year, daily rate truncated to seven decimals.

5. The closed-window calendar

Every closed window of the Fedwire Funds Service in the year, on nominal Eastern schedule hours: it opens 21:00 on the calendar day before each business day and closes 19:00, so a window between business days lasts 24 hours per calendar day less 22. The peak debit's median scales with hours / 50, or its square root.

Method: the formulas used, in the notation of Chapter 6

Four terms, R7 and R9

R7 = credit + access + s × enc + frictions,   at the low and high ends; base = (low + high) / 2
R9 = credit + access + s × (loan spread + bill − IORB × 1[balance earns IORB]) + frictions

s is the always-on share (R7) and the prefunded share (R9), compared at the same share as in the routes engine. Lendable share is 1 − s, and S2 passes when it is at least the bar. Bills positioned are s / (1 − haircut); the haircut gross-up is not priced again (it sits in frictions) and appears here only as a memo on the lendable share.

Crossover

s* = (aR7 − aR9) / (bR9 − bR7),   base(s) = a + b s

Both base prices are linear in the share, so they cross once; below s* R9 is cheaper. If R9's intercept and slope are both lower, R9 is cheaper at every share; a negative s* means R7 is cheaper at every positive share.

Sunday settlement cash

(a): + 0;   (b1): + s × enc;   (b2): + s × (loan spread + bill − IORB);   lendable (b1), (b2) = 1 − 2s

Reserves in a master account earn IORB, so case (b2) always nets IORB, whatever the joint-account switch. The mixed source takes a share m of the Sunday capacity from the chosen case and the rest from loans already pledged: price = R7 + m × extra, lendable = 1 − s − m s, linear in m as in the engine's tie, the m at which R9's base equals R7's: m* = (R9 − R7) / extrabase.

overdraft fee = 52 × s E[min(X, 1)] × floor(fee / 360, 7 dp) × 15 / 24,   E[min(X, c)] = m eσ²/2 Φ(−d − σ) + c Φ(d),  d = ln(m/c)/σ

Cap binding, one member

X = peak net debit / tier ~ lognormal(median mB / s, σ);   P(bind) = Φ(−ln(s / mB) / σ);   per year 1 − (1 − P)52

mB is the median weekend peak net debit as a share of the balance (the engines carry it as 25 percent of a 20 percent tier, 5 percent of the balance). The cap ladder holds mB fixed and moves the backing: backing = mB exp(σ z1−p) for a target p, priced with R7's four terms at that share, Sunday cash case (a). The median boundary is (1 − bar) / exp(σ z0.99), and (1 − bar)(1 − haircut) / exp(σ z0.99) counting the grossed-up bills.

R9 without top-up: d = ln(mB / s) / σ;  P(rejected) = Φ(d);  E[rejected] = mB eσ²/2 Φ(d + σ) − s Φ(d)

Same weekend across members

Zi = √ρ F + √(1 − ρ) ei;   number binding | factor ~ Binomial(M, p(factor))

Each member keeps its lognormal marginal, so its own bind rate does not depend on the copula; the copula changes how binds arrive together. Gaussian: p(z) = Φ((Φ−1(P) − √ρ z) / √(1 − ρ)). Student-t(5): p(z, W) = Φ((c √(W/5) − √ρ z) / √(1 − ρ)), W ~ χ²5, with c set so the marginal is P. The counts are computed by quadrature over the factor, as in the engine, on grids coarser than the engine's for speed: 2,001 points on [−8, 8] for the Gaussian (engine 4,001), and 161 × 120 in (z, W) for the Student-t (engine 401 × 240), W on a log grid from 10−4 to 80. The joint cap ladder solves for the per-member rate at which P(at least k of M) equals the target, by bisection on its logarithm between 10−10 and 0.6 (36 steps, as in the engine), and converts it to a backing as above. A quarter is ⌈M/4⌉. Every multi-member statistic describes an open debtor cohort, members drawn as net debtors with receivers outside the modelled set, not a closed membership.

Closed-window calendar

hours = 24 × (days from business day a to b) − 22;   Pwin(h, backing) = Φ(−ln(backing / (mB g(h))) / σ),  g = h/50 or √(h/50)

Business days: Monday to Friday less the Reserve Bank weekday closings of 2026 and 2027 (today); Sunday to Friday with weekday holidays open (the announced 22x6 schedule, not yet in effect); every day (seven days, hypothetical). Each business day is preceded by one window, and a window belongs to the year of the business day that ends it. P1 holds the backing at the share all year; P2 sizes it so the longest window binds no more often than a normal weekend under P1: backing = s g(hmax). "Sized to binds a year" finds by bisection (80 steps on 10−4 to 1) the backing at which the expected binds a year equal the routes engine's 52 × P(bind). Expected binds a year are the sum of Pwin over the windows; P(at least one) = 1 − Π(1 − Pwin). Seven days: with Fedwire Securities and repo still on weekdays, the pass needs weekend Reserve Bank credit, or reserves of s g(h) held for h = 50 or 65.25 hours (Friday 15:15 to Monday 8:30), which count as not lendable.

By deposit type

R8 = R6 + R7 − saving,  saving(x) = x × min(R6 access, R7 access low) + 1 bp for x > 0, and 0 at x = 0;   low x = 1, base 0.5, high 0;  lendable = 1 − 3% − s

R6 (the mutual with a Treasury-backed tail, reference design) and the strict R5 row are fixed inputs from the mutual-tail and clearing-cost engines and do not move with this page's inputs: R6 21.03 bps at a lendable share of 0.97 (3 percent capital contribution), R6 access term 0.37 bps; R5 sized to the S5 standard 37.94 at 1.00. The cheapest pairing for both clocks is the lower base of R8 (with that Sunday cash case) and R6 + R9.

What this page does not do

  • The queued amounts, the cohort-average secured debit and the pooled two-member cap come from the engine's Monte Carlo; the page shows the published values at the base case and does not recompute them.
  • No liquidity-buffer (analysis C), sponsored non-member (D) or Monday cash (E) calculations, and no elapsed-hours (daylight-saving) recomputation; those are in the engine's results.
  • Weekends are independent of each other; payment paths and the release of queued payments are not modelled.
  • The Student-t degrees of freedom are fixed at 5, the engine's value; the 52 weekends a year and the overdraft hours are fixed.

The JavaScript on this page is tested against the settlement and routes engines on the base case and nine varied parameter sets (prices within 0.05 basis points, probabilities within 2 percent relative).
